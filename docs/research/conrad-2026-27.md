## Required Innovation Stage package

| Component | Requirement | What judges see |
|---|---|---|
| Innovation Brief | Ten questions, combined maximum **3,000 words** | The story, innovation, progress, market, and business model. It is described as the most important submission item. |
| Innovation Video | **3–5 minutes**, MP4 under 800 MB, direct YouTube or Vimeo link (public or unlisted) | A tour of an innovation model: sketch, 3D model, storyboard, wireframe, prototype, or other visual artifact. |
| Website | Publicly accessible direct link; may be hidden from search engines | Team story, innovation model, brand, feasibility, and intended impact. |
| Reference attachment | A list of references cited in the Innovation Brief | Sources may include websites, videos, news, scientific papers, and interviews. |
| Optional attachments | Up to **two** | Visual aids or resources that supplement the brief. They are optional, not a substitute for required evidence. |

## Judging weights

| Weight | Criterion | Guide's question |
|---|---|---|
| **30%** | Theme / Innovation | Is it unique? Is it impactful? |
| **20%** | Storytelling and Professionalism | Would a reasonable investor want to learn more? |
| **20%** | Practicality | Will it work? |
| **20%** | Marketing Strategy | Does the team understand key markets? |
| **10%** | Finances | Does the team understand costs and funding? |

The scoring guide expands Practicality to include feasibility, realism, proof of concept, evidence base, and next steps. A concept-only submission with no tested component, prototype, demo, or credible feasibility evidence is weak on that criterion. This is a direct constraint on AquaInSAR: the repository currently has no processing run, pilot dataset, or validation. The competition case must therefore show an honest, reproducible feasibility test or a documented no-go, not an implied capability.

## Category question

The Challenge lists five categories, including:

- **The Water Challenge:** "helping communities worldwide secure safe and sustainable water sources. Student solutions should include: innovations to improve water equity, efficiency, and purity."
- **Aerospace and Aviation:** the Student Guide gives examples such as improving storage efficiency aboard the International Space Station and an autonomous magnetic takeoff and landing system. It does not give a one-sentence definition in the extracted text.

The repository [`README.md`](../../README.md) currently says the team is in **Aerospace & Aviation**. That is a recorded project description, not proof that the category was confirmed in the Conrad portal.

### Fit assessment

| Category | Plausible AquaInSAR case | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Water Challenge | The user problem is groundwater stress and water management. Satellite deformation is an input to a water decision. The category explicitly covers safe, sustainable water sources, equity, efficiency, and purity. | The current product is a monitoring/decision-support concept, not a water treatment or supply technology. The team must show a water-management decision that changes and evidence that someone needs it. |
| Aerospace & Aviation | The method uses spaceborne radar and Earth observation. The category can accommodate space technology and remote sensing. | The problem and customer are water, not aerospace or aviation. A radar-method story alone may look like a technology in search of a category. |

### Decision status

**Unconfirmed.** The Student Guide says teams select an innovation and category during Activation and choose the category that best fits the innovation. It does not describe a category-change process in the extracted text. Before assuming a change is possible:

1. Check the current Rules and Regulations and Terms.
2. Ask Conrad whether the registered category can be changed and by when.
3. Record the answer and the registered category in the project decision log.
4. Do not change the repository category, competition narrative, or pilot scope unilaterally.

If the team stays in Aerospace & Aviation, the narrative should make the spaceborne-radar capability and Earth-observation value explicit without inventing an aviation use. If the team moves to Water Challenge, the narrative should make the water decision, user, and equity/efficiency/purity benefit explicit without overstating scientific validation.

## Submission checklist

- [ ] Confirm the registered category and the portal's exact cutoff and timezone.
- [ ] Confirm payment status and financial-aid status.
- [ ] Freeze the brief at or below 3,000 words and check each question's limit.
- [ ] Attach a complete reference list with URLs, DOIs, and access dates.
- [ ] Label every technical claim as planned, observed, associated, validated, or predicted.
- [ ] Include a limitations/uncertainty section; do not imply well-level prediction.
- [ ] Show the innovation model in the video, not only slides.
- [ ] Verify the video and website links are public or unlisted and accessible without a team login.
- [ ] Check the MP4 is under 800 MB and the video is within 3–5 minutes.
- [ ] Confirm the website states the team, innovation, feasibility, and intended impact.
- [ ] Keep the optional attachments within the two-attachment limit.
- [ ] Recheck the official pages after submission and monitor email for Conrad questions.
